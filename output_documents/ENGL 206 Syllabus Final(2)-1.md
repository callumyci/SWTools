The University of Alberta acknowledges that we are located on Treaty 6
territory, and respects the histories, languages, and cultures of the
First Nations, Métis, Inuit, and all First Peoples of Canada, whose
presence continues to enrich our vibrant community.

Course Description: This course will provide an introduction to a
diverse range of poets and poetries with a focus on poetic forms, modes
of production and circulation, and contexts of reception in order to
think about ways poetry produces meaning and value. We'll read some
conventional and canonical poetry alongside innovative and experimental
works, examples of poetry in popular culture and various media, and
we'll do so with an attention to the work of creative individuals across
diverse cultures, histories, and geographies. We'll develop some
terminology for talking about poetry, and we'll explore strategies for
thinking about poetic meaning and where, and how, it might be located.

## Participation

The 10% grade assigned to participation is based on evident preparation
for class, completion of tasks during class, and contributions to group
work and class discussion.

## In-Class Writing Assignments

Students will be called upon to do some low-stakes in-class handwriting
on five unscheduled occasions during the term. The work will be
submitted for review (but not grading), and each submission is worth 1%
of the 5% allotted to in-class writing assignments. If you miss one of
these days without a valid excuse, the marks cannot be regained.

## Midterm Examination

For this assignment, you'll sit a Midterm Examination on Thursday,
February 16 during regular class time. You'll be asked to read and
analyze a short poem using some of the reading strategies and
terminology we've modelled and developed so far. The answer will consist
of a 500-750 word hand-written essay requiring careful close reading and
analysis of a single poem, without the use of secondary sources. You'll
have several poems from which to choose, none of which we will have
previously considered.

## Poetry Reading Report

Students will be required to attend one poetry reading on campus, off
campus, or virtually and reflect on that event in the form of a 250 word
written report. Each week, in person and on eClass, the instructor will
advise students of upcoming poetry readings to provide multiple
opportunities to fulfill this assignment. In the report, the student
will be asked to consider any relevant aspect of the event (venue,
audience composition and response, poet's performance style, the
materials performed) in evaluating the experience of that particular
poetry reading. Reports will not be accepted after Thursday, March 2.

## Close Listening Exercise

For this assignment, you'll be asked to select one of a number of short
recorded poetry performances, listen closely (and perhaps repeatedly) to
that recording, and reflect on a number of questions, or prompts,
designed to get you thinking about the difference between experiencing
the poem in print and experiencing the poem aurally. We'll consider how
the poem shifts in emphasis and meaning from the page to the stage, how
a listening audience differs from a reading audience, and what listening
might tell us that reading does not. This 500 word assignment will be
due on Thursday, March 16.

## Research Paper

Topics for the research paper will be distributed at the beginning of
March. The assignment will be due on Thursday, March 30. The essay will
be 1500 words in length and will require the use of a minimum of two
secondary sources in addition to your primary text(s).

## Student Responsibilities

Academic Integrity: The University of Alberta is committed to the
highest standards of academic integrity and honesty. Students are
expected to be familiar with these standards regarding academic honesty
and to uphold the policies of the University in this respect. Students
are particularly urged to familiarize themselves with the provisions of
the Code of Student Behaviour (COSB) and avoid any behaviour that could
potentially result in suspicions of cheating, plagiarism,
misrepresentation of facts and/or participation in an offence. Academic
dishonesty is a serious offence and can result in suspension or
expulsion from the University. Instructors are required to report
suspicion of violations to the COSB where investigation and disciplinary
action will follow. See the Academic Discipline Process.

All students should consult the Academic Integrity website. If you have
any questions, ask your instructor.

Plagiarism : plagiarism is the unacknowledged incorporation of work that
is not your own in an assignment that is submitted under your name: keep
in mind that plagiarism includes the uncited quotation of work that is
not your own, even if it is only a matter of a few words or sentences;
the paraphrasing or rewording of work that is not your own; the
inclusion of work in another writer's words, even when its information
seems to be 'common knowledge'; and, of course, the submission of an
essay that has not been written by you. Plagiarism is a serious matter,
and students are reminded to attend to careful quotation and
documentation in their written work and to be aware of the hazards of
too much help from tutors.

Recording of Lectures: Audio or video recording, digital or otherwise,
of lectures, labs, seminars or any other teaching environment by
students is allowed only with the prior written consent of the
instructor or as a part of an approved accommodation plan. Student or
instructor content, digital or otherwise, created and/or used within the
context of the course is to be used solely for personal study, and is
not to be used or distributed for any other purpose without prior
written consent from the content author(s).
